## H2：什么是 PVD 真空镀？

PVD 是在低压真空环境中形成薄膜的一组工艺。镀层材料从固体源释放，经真空空间传输后在基材表面凝结。常见能量来源和沉积机制包括电阻或电子束蒸发、磁控溅射以及阴极电弧。加入氮、氧等反应气体时，还可形成项目指定的化合物薄膜。

PVD 与传统湿式电镀的核心区别在于沉积环境和材料传输方式。PVD 不依赖把零件浸入金属盐电镀液完成沉积，但前处理、清洁、装夹、真空度、温度和工艺稳定性同样决定结果。

对装饰件而言，PVD 可在合适的基材和底层上形成连续的金属质感或颜色。对功能件而言，可选用硬质或低摩擦薄膜改善表面性能。两类目标不能只用颜色名称代替技术要求：装饰件应定义色样、光泽、可见面和使用环境；功能件应定义膜层体系、厚度、硬度、附着力、摩擦、耐磨或其他验收指标。

## H2：Xmake 提供的 PVD 工艺路线

### H3：装饰真空金属化

装饰真空金属化常用于需要亮银、金属镜面或半反射效果的塑料与金属零件。塑料件通常需要底涂、表面活化或等离子处理，以改善表面平整度和附着力；沉积后可根据耐刮、颜色和环境要求增加透明或着色保护面层。

适用方向：消费产品外壳、装饰盖、旋钮、标牌、照明反射部件和展示件。  
控制重点：塑料牌号、脱模剂残留、底涂兼容性、热负荷、挂点、边缘覆盖和面层耐久性。

### H3：磁控溅射

磁控溅射利用等离子体轰击靶材，使靶材原子或粒子释放并沉积到零件表面。该路线适合需要较稳定薄膜组成、装饰颜色、金属导电层或光学 / 反射功能的项目，也可用于金属、选定塑料、玻璃和陶瓷。

适用方向：精密外观件、电子部件、光学与反射部件、薄金属层和多层薄膜。  
控制重点：靶材、反应气体、基材温度、膜层均匀性、装夹旋转、遮蔽和批次色差。

### H3：阴极电弧与离子镀

阴极电弧和离子镀适合在金属零件上形成结合力较强的装饰或功能薄膜。可按项目开发金色、黑色、枪色等装饰外观，也可形成以耐磨、硬度或低摩擦为重点的硬质薄膜。

适用方向：五金件、工具部件、模具零件、耐磨组件和高接触频率装饰件。  
控制重点：基材硬度、回火稳定性、沉积温度、表面粗糙度、刃口状态、膜层体系和使用载荷。

### H3：反应性与多层 PVD

反应性 PVD 在沉积过程中引入工艺气体，形成氮化物、氧化物或其他项目指定薄膜。多层或梯度结构可用于平衡附着力、颜色、硬度、内应力和表面功能。

适用方向：指定颜色、硬质保护、耐磨、低摩擦和多功能表面。  
控制重点：基材兼容性、膜层结构、总厚度、界面结合、热影响和测试标准。

玻璃和陶瓷可用于装饰、反射、导电或其他薄膜项目，但需要确认清洁度、热膨胀、附着、装夹和边缘风险。透明基材还应定义观察方向、透过率、反射率和颜色要求。

## H2：PVD 的优势与设计限制

### 优势

- 外观范围广：可获得从镜面金属感到哑光、拉丝和彩色薄膜的多种效果。
- 薄膜尺寸影响小：适合需要保留几何精度的零件，但关键配合仍需图纸控制。
- 可兼顾装饰与功能：不同膜层可服务于颜色、反射、耐磨、硬度、低摩擦或导电目标。
- 可覆盖多类基材：金属、选定塑料、玻璃和陶瓷可采用不同前处理与沉积路线。
- 工艺整合：Xmake 可将零件制造、表面准备、PVD、后处理和检验纳入同一交付流程。

### 设计与工艺限制

- PVD 主要是视线可达工艺。深孔、狭窄内腔、背面和遮挡区的沉积量可能明显降低。
- 薄膜会复制原始表面。深划痕、孔隙、缩痕、刀纹和焊接缺陷不会被自动遮盖。
- 尖锐边缘、薄壁和复杂装夹会影响局部温度、膜厚、挂点和外观一致性。
- 螺纹、压配合、轴承位、密封面和电接触面应在图纸中标出是否遮蔽。
- 塑料件需要评估耐温、放气、脱模剂、涂层附着和底涂体系。
- 功能膜的性能取决于完整组合：基材、前处理、膜层体系、厚度、载荷和环境缺一不可。

## H2：常见问题

### PVD 真空镀和电镀有什么区别？

PVD 在真空中把固体源材料转化并沉积成薄膜；电镀通常在电解液中通过电化学反应沉积金属。两者在适用基材、膜厚、覆盖方式、颜色、性能和前处理方面不同，应按零件目标选择。

### 塑料可以做 PVD 吗？

可以。选定塑料可通过清洗、底涂、等离子活化和真空金属化形成金属外观。需要确认树脂牌号、填料、耐温、放气、脱模剂残留、附着和保护面层。

### PVD 可以做哪些颜色？

常见方向包括银色、金色、香槟金、玫瑰金、铜色、青铜色、枪色和黑色。实际颜色由膜层体系、基材纹理、光泽和面层决定，量产应使用批准色样。

### PVD 会影响零件尺寸吗？

PVD 是薄膜沉积，尺寸影响通常小于较厚的喷涂或部分电镀体系，但不能视为零影响。精密配合、螺纹、密封面、孔径和轴承位仍需定义膜厚与遮蔽。

### PVD 能覆盖深孔和内腔吗？

覆盖受视线、零件旋转、装夹和几何影响。深孔、狭窄内腔、背面和遮挡区域的膜厚可能低于外表面，需要在图纸评审时确认。

### PVD 表面是否耐刮耐磨？

取决于基材、膜层类型、厚度、附着、表面硬度、载荷和环境。装饰金属化与硬质功能 PVD 不是同一性能等级，应按用途定义测试。

### 报价需要提供什么？

请提供 2D / 3D 图纸、材料与牌号、数量、目标颜色或实物色样、可见面、遮蔽区域、关键公差、膜层功能、使用环境和测试要求。塑料件还应说明树脂、填料和现有表面状态。

## H2: What Is PVD Coating?

PVD is a family of thin-film processes performed in a low-pressure vacuum. Coating material is released from a solid source, transported through the vacuum, and condensed on the substrate. Common energy sources and deposition mechanisms include resistance or electron-beam evaporation, magnetron sputtering, and cathodic arc. When process gases such as nitrogen or oxygen are introduced, project-defined compound films can also be formed.

The principal difference between PVD and conventional wet electroplating is the deposition environment and material-transfer mechanism. PVD does not rely on immersing the part in a metal-salt plating bath, but pretreatment, cleanliness, fixturing, vacuum conditions, temperature, and process stability are still decisive.

For decorative parts, PVD can create a continuous metallic appearance or color on a suitable substrate and base layer. For functional parts, hard or low-friction films can improve surface behavior. These goals need different acceptance criteria. Decorative work should define the color master, gloss, visible surfaces, and service environment. Functional work should define the coating system, thickness, hardness, adhesion, friction, wear, or other test metrics.

## H2: PVD Processes We Offer

### H3: Decorative Vacuum Metallizing

Decorative vacuum metallizing is used for plastic and metal parts requiring bright silver, mirror-like metallic, or semi-reflective effects. Plastic parts commonly need a basecoat, surface activation, or plasma treatment to improve smoothness and adhesion. A clear or tinted protective topcoat can be added after deposition when scratch resistance, color, or environmental durability requires it.

Typical uses: consumer housings, decorative covers, knobs, nameplates, lighting reflectors, and display components.

### H3: Magnetron Sputtering

Magnetron sputtering uses plasma to bombard a target, releasing atoms or particles that deposit on the part. It is suitable for projects requiring controlled film composition, decorative color, a metallic conductive layer, or optical / reflective behavior, and can be used on metals, selected plastics, glass, and ceramics.

Typical uses: precision appearance parts, electronic components, optical and reflective components, thin metallic layers, and multilayer films.  
Key controls: target material, reactive gas, substrate temperature, film uniformity, part rotation, masking, and batch color variation.

### H3: Cathodic Arc and Ion Plating

Cathodic arc and ion plating are used to form strongly bonded decorative or functional films on metal parts. Projects may target gold, black, gunmetal, and other decorative appearances, or hard films focused on wear, hardness, or low friction.

Typical uses: hardware, tool components, mold parts, wear components, and frequently handled decorative parts.  
Key controls: substrate hardness, tempering stability, deposition temperature, surface roughness, edge condition, coating architecture, and operating load.

### H3: Reactive and Multilayer PVD

Reactive PVD introduces process gases during deposition to form nitrides, oxides, or other project-defined films. Multilayer or graded structures can balance adhesion, color, hardness, internal stress, and surface function.

Typical uses: specified colors, hard protection, wear resistance, low friction, and multifunctional surfaces.  
Key controls: substrate compatibility, coating architecture, total thickness, interface bonding, thermal influence, and test standard.

### Design and Process Limits

- Threads, press fits, bearing seats, sealing faces, and electrical contacts should be identified for masking on the drawing.
- Plastic parts require review of temperature resistance, outgassing, mold release, coating adhesion, and basecoat compatibility.
- Functional performance comes from the complete system: substrate, pretreatment, film architecture, thickness, load, and environment.

## H2: Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between PVD and electroplating?

PVD converts material from a solid source and deposits it as a film in a vacuum. Electroplating normally deposits metal electrochemically in an electrolyte. They differ in suitable substrates, thickness, coverage behavior, color, performance, and pretreatment, so selection should follow the part requirements.

### Can plastic parts receive PVD coating?

Yes. Selected plastics can receive a metallic appearance through cleaning, basecoating, plasma activation, and vacuum metallizing. Resin grade, filler, heat resistance, outgassing, mold-release residue, adhesion, and protective topcoat must be considered.

### What colors are available for PVD?

Common directions include silver, gold, champagne, rose gold, copper, bronze, gunmetal, and black. Actual color depends on coating architecture, substrate texture, gloss, and topcoat. Production should use an approved physical master.

### Does PVD change part dimensions?

PVD is a thin-film process and normally has less dimensional impact than thicker paint or some plating systems, but it is not zero. Precision fits, threads, sealing faces, hole diameters, and bearing seats still require thickness and masking definition.

### Can PVD coat deep holes and internal cavities?

Coverage depends on line of sight, part rotation, fixturing, and geometry. Film thickness in deep holes, narrow cavities, back sides, and shadowed areas can be lower than on exposed surfaces and should be reviewed during drawing assessment.

### Is a PVD surface scratch- and wear-resistant?

That depends on the substrate, coating type, thickness, adhesion, surface hardness, load, and environment. Decorative vacuum metallizing and hard functional PVD do not have the same performance level; testing must follow the intended use.

### What information is needed for a quotation?

Provide 2D / 3D drawings, material and grade, quantity, target color or physical master, visible faces, masked areas, critical tolerances, film function, service environment, and test requirements. For plastics, include resin, filler, and current surface condition.
