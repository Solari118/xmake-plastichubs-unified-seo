## 3. High-Conversion Needs

- Find a plastic CNC machining supplier that can quote from CAD files.
- Compare supplier capabilities: materials, tolerances, process range, quality and lead time.
- Validate whether CNC machining, 3D printing, vacuum casting or injection molding is the right process.
- Get prototype and low-volume production support before tooling or mass production.
- Source precision machined plastic parts for automotive, medical, industrial equipment, electronics, IoT and robotics.
- Prepare RFQ files and drawings so engineering and procurement can get a reliable quote.

## 4. Decision-Support Needs

- Material selection: POM vs Nylon, PEEK vs POM, ABS vs PC, PMMA vs PC.
- Process selection: CNC vs 3D printing, CNC vs injection molding, vacuum casting vs injection molding.
- Tolerance and DFM: how plastic material behavior affects machining, surface finish, thin walls, warping, threads and inserts.
- Production stage: prototype, pilot run, low-volume, repeat production and high-volume molding.
- Quality: inspection report, traceability, certificates, industry-specific standards.

## 5. Low-Priority Or Weak-Fit Audiences

- Hobbyist 3D printing users seeking ultra-cheap single printed objects.
- Broad consumer searches for plastic products with no CAD/RFQ or custom manufacturing intent.
- Metal CNC / sheet metal searches unless they clearly support plastic assembly or enclosure context.
- Generic “what is plastic” or school-level glossary searches with weak buying intent.
