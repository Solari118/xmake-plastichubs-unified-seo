# SEO/GEO Planning Rules

## Keyword Strategy

- Treat the primary keyword as the central user topic, not a repetition quota.
- Map natural variants, expert terminology, buyer modifiers, process/material entities, and task questions.
- Recommend exact-match usage only where it reads naturally, usually in the title or H1 when it accurately describes the page.
- Do not require the same keyword in the first paragraph, an H2, FAQ, and body.
- Do not invent search volume, CPC, difficulty, ranking, or traffic potential.

## Content Diversity

Choose structure from intent and evidence. Useful patterns include:

- failure diagnosis and corrective action
- engineering decision matrix
- process or procurement workflow
- material/process comparison
- standards or specification explanation
- design-rule guide
- case-led analysis
- myth/risk correction

Do not label these patterns in public copy. Do not reuse one pattern for every article. FAQ, tables, checklists, calculators, and diagrams are conditional tools, not mandatory blocks.

## Intent, Section, And Readability Design

- Convert the primary intent into observable completion criteria, not a broad label such as `informational`.
- Identify the earliest point where the core answer must appear. A long article can still be intent-first when the decision is resolved early and deeper sections are optional.
- Assign one reader job to every H2. If a decision table and later “when to choose” sections repeat the same criteria, consolidate them under one decision module.
- Use headings that name the buyer task, decision, defect, material, or process criterion. Avoid generic speed labels such as `Quick Answer`, `Fast Guide`, or `Rapid Decision` unless speed itself is the query subject.
- Carry user-specified minimum length and readability targets into the brief. Do not use word count as a proxy for completeness and do not impose a universal minimum.
- For global English audiences, prefer plain, direct sentences and define unfamiliar technical terms before relying on them. A numeric grade target applies only when supplied or explicitly selected.

## GEO Answer Planning

- Identify the direct questions an engineer or buyer would ask.
- Break complex topics into fan-out subquestions.
- Plan concise answer units with conditions and limitations.
- Prefer primary sources, official standards, direct company data, and traceable technical evidence for internal verification.
- Store external source names and URLs only in internal evidence fields marked `Internal — Not for Publication`. Do not convert evidence traceability into public third-party attribution or outbound links.
- Plan statistics or quotations only when exact sources exist.
- Clarify entities, service scope, materials, processes, and brand ownership.
- Do not promise AI citations, invent special AI markup, or treat keyword stuffing as GEO.

## Commercial Relevance

- `none`: no supplier-model comparison.
- `soft`: connect one technical risk to factory-direct control, DFM, inspection, quoting, traceability, or accountability.
- `explicit`: compare sourcing models because the query itself concerns suppliers, brokers, marketplaces, quotes, or procurement risk.

Commercial language must remain factual, specific, and proportional to the reader's task.

## Public Link Boundary

- Plan same-brand internal links for public content.
- Do not plan third-party public links or attribution by default.
- If external attribution is necessary for a claim, mark the claim `Needs approval` instead of silently publishing the source.
- Paid placements require explicit commercial approval and `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`.
