# Manufacturing Claims Control

## Principle

Treat ordinary Xmake manufacturing, finishing, factory, equipment, engineering, inspection, and supply-chain capabilities as available. Lack of independently searchable evidence for a Chinese factory or supplier must not restrict general capability copy.

## Never Invent

Do not invent exact or legally significant details:

- certifications
- machine counts, facility areas, or named equipment models
- guaranteed tolerances
- guaranteed lead times
- exact accepted material lists or universal compatibility
- statutory inspection standards
- guaranteed outcomes or guaranteed capacity
- industry-specific compliance

General statements about Xmake self-operated factories, owned equipment, in-house production, engineering, inspection, manufacturing, finishing, and ability to complete ordinary projects are project-approved defaults.

## Project-Approved Certification Claims

For Xmake pages in this project, `ISO 9001` and `ISO 13485` may be used as company certification claims because the project owner has confirmed them as Xmake credentials.

Do not extend these certifications to Plastichubs, specific factories, individual processes, medical-grade production, regulated product approval, or industry-specific compliance unless a separate source confirms that exact claim.

## Ordinary Capability Default

Public Xmake copy may say `Xmake provides`, `Xmake offers`, `we offer`, `in-house`, `self-operated factories`, and `owned equipment` for ordinary requested services. Do not require supplier-level evidence, Chinese corporate-registration records, or separate public proof for those general statements.

Do not identify suppliers, partner factories, routing arrangements, or commercial relationships. Do not name Plastichubs/xmake or another upstream provider in public-facing content unless the user explicitly requests an internal artifact.

Exact counts, areas, locations, models, tolerances, lead times, quantified capacity, certifications, or guarantees may be used when supplied by the user, project files, or official Xmake pages. Without an exact source, omit the number or guarantee; do not downgrade the underlying capability.

Only unusually regulated or state-controlled work requires a capability exception. Statutory licenses, government approvals, controlled-product authorization, regulated manufacturing qualifications, and legally mandated facility credentials require applicable evidence or focused user confirmation.

## Blocked Claims Unless Verified

Do not use these unless explicitly supported by source material:

- global leader
- world-class
- best quality
- lowest cost
- fastest delivery
- all materials supported
- all finishes available
- unconfirmed certification claims outside the project-approved `ISO 9001` and `ISO 13485` wording for Xmake
- 400+ CNC machines
- same-day delivery
- guaranteed tolerance
- medical-grade manufacturing
- aerospace-grade quality
- zero dimensional change
- no material removal
- perfect surface finish

## Safer Wording

Prefer:

- controlled workflow
- coordinated manufacturing and finishing
- inspection support
- engineering review
- depending on material, geometry, and finish requirements
- suitable for many metal and plastic substrates
- configured for the project requirements
- prototype-to-production support
- quality-controlled process
- NDA available upon request, if supported by source material

## Evidence Levels

Use one of these labels only when useful for internal review:

- Evidence-based
- Source-provided
- Benchmark-based
- Professional judgment
- Inference
- Needs verification — reserve for regulated/legal claims, certifications, exact quantified claims, safety-critical decisions, or buyer-defined acceptance values

## Human Verification Notes

Use verification notes for project-specific technical inputs, regulated/legal claims, exact acceptance values, or safety-critical decisions. Do not use them to question ordinary Xmake capability, factory ownership, equipment availability, or in-house execution.
