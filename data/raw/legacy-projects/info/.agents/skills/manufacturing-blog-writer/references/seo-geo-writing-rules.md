# SEO/GEO Writing Rules

## People And Task First

- Complete the engineer's or buyer's task before optimizing presentation.
- Treat `INTENT_COMPLETION_CRITERIA` as acceptance tests. Answer the core question in the opening or first core H2 so the reader does not need to finish the article to obtain the decision.
- Add information beyond generic summaries: conditions, failure modes, selection logic, evidence, examples, or decision consequences.
- State practical go/no-go boundaries when the reader is choosing a process, material, supplier type, or production path.
- Name concrete failure modes and connect DFM guidance to a defect, rework risk, inspection failure, or schedule consequence.
- Include traceability, inspection, material records, certification, or standards context only when it changes selection, acceptance, or compliance decisions.
- Do not write to a fixed word count.
- When `MIN_WORD_COUNT` is user-specified, meet it with non-redundant decision value. When absent, do not pad.

## Natural Keyword Coverage

- Preserve the supplied primary keyword when it is used as an SEO field.
- Use exact match only where natural and semantically correct.
- Cover query variants, entities, questions, and expert terminology according to meaning.
- Avoid density targets, position checklists, repeated anchors, and keyword-shaped headings that reduce readability.
- Keep internal-link anchors accurate and naturally varied; exact-match repetition is not an SEO requirement.

## Flexible Structure

- Select headings and order from the topic's reasoning needs.
- Give each H2 one distinct reader job. Merge a table, checklist, and later sections when they repeat the same selection logic.
- Prefer headings that name the decision, defect, material, process, or buyer task. Avoid generic `Quick Answer`, `Fast Guide`, and `Rapid Decision` labels unless speed is the actual subject.
- Use FAQ only when real query questions remain after the main article.
- Do not force FAQ or a fixed conclusion/FAQ sequence. When both appear, follow the approved brief and keep the CTA inside the concluding decision or action module rather than as a detached sales line.
- Use a table only for comparison or structured lookup.
- Use a checklist for action verification, not as decoration.
- Use diagrams or tools only when they reduce explanation cost or decision error.
- Vary paragraph length and transitions naturally.
- Make adjacent paragraphs advance through engineering cause, condition, consequence, or decision. Avoid a stack of disconnected micro-answers.
- Keep public paragraphs to three sentences maximum. Vary sentence length and evidence form so the rule does not create mechanical rhythm.
- For comparison topics, organize core sections by shared buyer criteria and keep both options visible. Do not publish long one-process explanations without stating their effect on the comparison.
- Use a consequence-led SPEAR opening and an answer-first decision aid when the query asks the reader to choose.

## Language And Readability

- Honor the supplied `READABILITY_TARGET`; do not invent a numeric target when none is provided.
- For broad international English, prefer direct syntax, moderate sentence length, and a definition before relying on an unfamiliar term.
- Write English as English. Do not preserve Chinese word order, punctuation, or connective patterns merely to keep line-by-line symmetry.
- Keep the Chinese review and English publication layers semantically aligned while allowing natural phrasing in each language.
- Keep a peer-to-peer B2B technical voice throughout. Replace vague phrases such as `ensure the quality`, `meet the requirements`, or `has a good performance` with the exact characteristic, acceptance criterion, or result.

## Anti-Fluff And Commercial Tone

- Start with the engineering or sourcing consequence, not broad industry context or unsupported brand leadership.
- Remove empty transitions, tautologies, vague industry-observer statements, and consecutive paragraph openings that repeat the same subject-plus-verb pattern.
- Do not lecture the reader with generic `should` or `must` statements. Direct imperatives are acceptable for necessary procedures, design checks, and safety controls.
- Replace praise and abstract actions with a process condition and physical or commercial result. Do not dramatize ordinary manufacturing risk into launch-destroying or catastrophic language.
- Keep sourcing-model comparisons factual. Do not use `broker`, `middleman`, `unverified shops`, `black box`, or `trap` as disparaging labels.

## Delivery And Artifact QA

- Keep review, preview, WordPress, and Word-ready stages distinct.
- Bilingual review drafts use stacked Markdown with no raw layout HTML.
- Paginated previews and Word files require page rendering and visual inspection of fonts, punctuation, headings, tables, links, and page breaks.
- If visual rendering cannot run, state that limitation and do not label the artifact visually verified.
- Apply wording or display corrections to the source artifact before reporting completion.

## GEO Support

- Make key answers explicit in text.
- Break complex decisions into useful subquestions.
- Define entities and distinguish similar processes, materials, standards, and brands.
- Attach conditions and limitations to technical claims.
- Keep primary or official evidence traceable in the private claim/source ledger. Do not publish third-party names, citations, or outbound links unless the user explicitly approved an exception.
- Use statistics and quotations only when sourced and relevant.
- Do not add special AI files, invented schema, or guaranteed visibility language.

## Publishing Readiness

- Provide accurate title, meta description, H1, URL/canonical status, author/reviewer status, dates, image requirements, same-brand internal links, private external evidence notes, and schema recommendation when applicable.
- Structured data must match visible content.
