# Audit Evaluation Cases

1. Strong technical article with no broker section and `COMMERCIAL_BRIDGE_MODE=none`: must not lose commercial points merely for omitting broker contrast.
2. Informational article with `soft`: should pass when factory-direct value is brief and contextual.
3. Supplier comparison with `explicit`: should lose points when sourcing-model comparison is absent or unsupported.
4. Article mentioning RapidDirect in metadata or CTA: hard fail and Red.
5. PlasticHubs article claiming metal die casting: hard fail and Red.
6. Accurate article using `typically` and `project-dependent`: must not fail trust solely for reasonable qualification.
7. Keyword-rich article with mechanical exact-match repetition: deduct SEO and structure points.
8. Final Blog with no visible FAQ or fewer than three useful non-duplicative answers: hard fail and Red even when the body otherwise completes intent.
9. Final article with required but missing Top-10 benchmark: hard fail and Red.
10. Article that adapts an approved reference's macro-structure but copies its distinctive headings, prose, proprietary examples, or full outline: hard fail even if technically accurate.
11. Article that executes the approved primary/multi-reference adaptation, validates it against the rest of the Top-10, and closes documented gaps: reward information gain and differentiated structure.
12. Best-effort benchmark transparently labeled and explicitly approved by the user: audit the limitations but do not fail merely because exact localization was unavailable. Missing downgrade approval evidence is a hard failure.
13. Comparison article repeats one selection matrix in separate “when to choose” sections: deduct structure and require consolidation even when every section is technically accurate.
14. Global-audience article with `READABILITY_TARGET=US grade 9-12` and `MIN_WORD_COUNT=2000`: verify both without rewarding filler; fail a final-labeled artifact that ignores either explicit requirement.
15. Non-speed query using `Quick Answer` and `Fast Decision Guide`: flag generic headings and require buyer-task labels; do not ban factual uses of speed elsewhere.
16. Word-ready claim without rendered page inspection: withhold full publishing readiness and report structural QA only.
17. Ordinary XMAKE service statement with no Chinese registry evidence: do not fail capability by default; continue to verify exact figures, certifications, guarantees, and regulated claims.
18. User requests local edits and states the original is better: audit whether sound source logic and voice were preserved; flag unnecessary full-article replacement.
19. Two recent same-brand articles repeat the same opening logic, H2 order, evidence form, CTA, and image logic without an intent-based reason: fail the cross-article similarity gate and return the affected article to Info.
20. A troubleshooting Blog may omit RFQ, a decision table, and DFM when its diagnostic flow completes intent, but it must still include a concise topic-specific FAQ and a visible actionable CTA.
21. Three articles use different headings and have zero 10-word shingle overlap but repeat the same semantic sequence, H2 count, section proportions, paragraph cadence, and supplier-input ending: fail the cross-article similarity gate and return them to Info.
22. Audit finds the selected intent, structure reference, or rhythm route was wrong: return to Plan, then replay Info and Audit. Audit finds execution, English, hierarchy, FAQ, CTA, or corpus-diversity defects: return to Info, repair, then re-run Audit. Neither route may skip directly to Final Output.
23. A brief bypasses an unresolved Google verification challenge or tool-permission block: hard fail and return to Plan; do not accept API candidates as exact Google positions.

Reviewers should compare decisions across cases to detect scoring drift.
