# **XMAKE TECHNICAL CAPABILITIES**

XMAKE is a custom manufacturing service provider founded in 2009, offering end-to-end solutions from rapid prototyping to mass production. Its core technical capabilities cover the following six areas:

| Capability Category | Key Processes/Technologies | Highlights |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| CNC Machining | 3/4/5-Axis Milling, Turning, Mill-Turn | High precision (±0.003 mm), fast delivery (as fast as 1 day), wide range of metals & plastics |
| Sheet Metal Fabrication | Laser Cutting, Bending, Welding, Stamping, Riveting | Large-format processing, multiple surface finishes, ISO 2768 standard tolerances |
| 3D Printing | SLA, SLS, FDM, MJF, SLM, PolyJet | Multi-material options (resin, nylon, metal, etc.), rapid turnaround (as fast as 1 day) |
| Injection Molding | Plastic Injection, Insert Molding, Overmolding, Mold Making | From prototype to production molds, mold life classification, mold flow analysis |
| Vacuum Casting | Silicone Molding, Polyurethane Casting | Ideal for low-volume complex parts, excellent surface finish, customizable colors/textures |
| Other Extended Processes | Die Casting, Aluminum Extrusion, Forging, Powder Metallurgy | Covers specialty process needs, provides material & process advice |

Furthermore, XMAKE provides:

* Full-cycle NPI (New Product Introduction) Services: From design review, prototype validation, pilot runs to mass production handover.  
* Assembly & Packaging Services: Including manual assembly, bonding, welding, and packaging design.  
* Surface Finishing: Anodizing, bead blasting, electroplating, powder coating, polishing, and dozens of other finishes.

* But in B2B, we still deal with **people** on the other side.

Therefore, XMAKE’s B2B user personas are split into:

* **Company persona**

* **Role (job position) persona**

* Mainly from Europe and North America, in developed countries.

* Mostly **small and medium-sized companies**, with **fewer than 100 employees**.

* Industries include **consumer products, industrial equipment, energy, medical devices, automotive**, and others.

### **2.2 User Roles and the Product Development Process**

Typical standardized flow:

* **Product Designer** handles appearance design.

* **Engineer** handles functional design.

* **Project Manager** oversees overall project schedule.

* **Purchasing** sources quotes from multiple manufacturing service providers.

* **CEO** approves the final purchasing decision.

In real life:

* The **smaller** the company, the **more** involvement and decision power the **CEO** has.

* In **mid-sized** companies, **engineers** often have relatively high decision power.

* The **larger** the company, the **more decisive** the **purchasing** role becomes.

---

* EVT – Engineering Validation and Testing

* DVT – Design Validation and Testing

* PVT – Production Validation and Testing

* MP – Mass Production

* EOL – End of Life

#### **Goals**

* Design systems and components that meet requirements.

* Run disciplined experiments, analyze data, and interpret results.

* Ensure production is **low risk** and **cost-effective**.

### **2.3.2 Purchasing**

* Purchasing roles account for **14%** of XMAKE’s existing customers, the **second largest** role.

* Common titles:

  * Buyer

  * Purchasing / Purchasing Specialist

  * Senior Sourcing Manager

  * Purchasing Manager

  * Sourcing Strategy Manager, etc.

#### **Responsibilities**

Purchasing is the organized acquisition of goods and services on behalf of the buying entity, ensuring required items are obtained on time and at reasonable cost.

In manufacturing companies, the purchasing department is especially critical because large volumes of raw materials and components must be acquired continuously.

#### **Typical Work**

* Source suppliers based on price, availability, necessity, quality, and logistics costs.

* Evaluate suppliers and product quality through visits, interviews, and analysis.

* Work with internal departments to ensure all customer requirements are met.

* Negotiate sales agreements and supply contracts in the company’s favor.

* Coordinate with logistics providers and sellers to ensure timely delivery, and supervise the completeness and quality of delivered goods and services.

* Approve payments and manage purchasing expenses with accurate records.

#### **Goals**

* Maximize value for money in purchasing.

* Ensure optimal quality.

* Research supplier reliability.

* Build strategic relationships.

* Improve purchasing efficiency.

#### **Pain Points**

1. Finding suitable suppliers.

2. Reducing costs.

3. Measuring supplier performance.

4. Tracking multiple markets at once.

5. Using limited time effectively.

### **2.3.4 Product Designer**

* Designers make up about **5%** of XMAKE’s existing customers. In the workflow, they are **upstream** of engineers.

* Common titles:

  * Designer

  * Mechanical Designer

  * Product Designer

  * Drafter, etc.

#### **Responsibilities**

Product designers are responsible for the design and development of consumer products. Their responsibilities include improving existing product designs and analyzing competing products in order to match or exceed their quality and performance.

#### **Typical Work**

* Create design concepts and drawings to determine optimal products.

* Present product ideas to relevant team members for brainstorming.

* Suggest improvements to product engineers to enhance design and performance.

* Turn design concepts into functional prototypes.

* Research technologies and product structures and incorporate them into design concepts.

* Stay informed on current industry trends and market conditions.

* Coordinate with other designers to ensure accurate and efficient communication at the design stage.

* Modify existing designs to meet customer expectations.

#### **Goals**

* Find a company that can turn their ideas into reality.

* Validate whether their design is manufacturable.

* Check specifications of certain materials.

* Meet targets set by their manager.

* Find the resources they need, and prioritize them in their design.

#### **Pain Points**

* Local services are too expensive.

* Foreign manufacturers may not accept small-batch orders.

* They do not know which material or surface finish best suits the design.
